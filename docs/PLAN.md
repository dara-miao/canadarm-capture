## The question

**When a contact-rich simulation gives a result, how do you tell whether it is physics or an artefact of how contact was modelled, and which modelling choices is the result most sensitive to?**

The capture is the case study, not the subject. The answer is meant to carry to any simulator and any task where a robot has to make and keep contact: a hand closing on a mug, a foot on gravel, a peg entering a hole. It has two parts:

1. *Checks that catch a result that is not physics.* Does energy appear from nowhere? Are contact forces far beyond anything the machine can produce? Does the result change when a surface is represented a different way?
2. *A ranking of what the result depends on.* Change one uncertain modelling choice at a time and measure how far the outcome moves.

The project already has one worked example of the first part. Until 2026-10-11 the model lost most snared pins in harsh conditions. Three physical explanations were written for it. All were wrong: the contact solver was failing on one pair of shapes, and changing how that one surface was represented took captures from 3 of 30 to 30 of 30.

The two earlier questions are kept for the record:

- *Which of the model's assumptions decide where the simulated capture fails?* This is now the second part of the question above, asked of this model.
- *Why do snared pins get lost?* Answered on 2026-10-11: a contact failure in the model.

## Why this scenario

Canadarm2 already captures cargo vehicles such as Cygnus, routinely. This project solves no problem for that operation, and its real limits may be fully characterised in documents that are not public. A failure in this model is a failure of this model.

The scenario is chosen because it is a real operation in which making contact is the whole difficulty. The target weighs seven tonnes and is free to move. In zero gravity nothing holds it still, so a touch in the wrong place pushes it away, next to a crewed station. Reaching the target is only the start: the arm has to make and keep contact with it. And zero gravity strips away almost everything except contact, which makes it a clean place to see what a simulator's contact model is doing.

There is also a personal aim. I am not from the space industry. I am building this to learn, by trying to replicate a real operation, everything a contact-rich simulation has to get right. My interest behind it is physical AI: robots are trained and tested in simulation, which only works if the simulated contact can be trusted.

The project is worthwhile if the model is transparent, the experiments are careful, the failures are explained, and the limits are stated. No literature gap is claimed in advance.

## What a contact-rich simulation has to account for

Every design decision in this project belongs to one of five layers.

| Layer | What has to be decided | Here |
|---|---|---|
| The world | What exists, its mass and where that mass sits, how parts are joined, gravity, what is fixed and what is free | `models/capture_base.xml`, `capture/scene.py` |
| Contact | The simple shapes used for collision, which pairs may touch, how overlap becomes force, friction | The same files. This is where the lost-pin failure was |
| The machine | Motors and their force limits and stiffness; mechanisms, and which are modelled and which are substitutes | Arm servos, snare drive, pull-in carriage, latch force |
| The brain | What the controller can sense, how late and how noisy; how it turns that into commands; when it gives up | `capture/sensing.py`, `capture/controller.py` |
| The experiment | Time step, repeatable scenarios, what counts as success, sanity checks, where each number comes from, what survives a change | `capture/trial.py`, `capture/batch.py`, `experiments/`, this plan |

The first four layers are where modelling choices live. The fifth is how to find out which of them a result depends on.

**Uncertainty.** Each number in the model is measured, required, borrowed, or guessed, and the tables below say which. A guessed number is treated like a measurement with a wide uncertainty in a physics lab: vary it across its plausible range and see how far the result moves. That is what the sensitivity experiments are.

Three habits from the uncertainty guide in my first-year physics course apply directly:

- *State a value with its uncertainty.* A capture rate from 12 attempts is not "50%"; one attempt either way is 8 points. Results should say how many attempts they rest on.
- *A measurement made once gets its uncertainty from how it was made.* That is every guessed number here: the joint rate cap, the friction, the plate's radius. Each needs a plausible range, not just a value.
- *The largest percentage uncertainty dominates.* A lab calculation finds this with a formula. A simulation has no formula, so the same thing is found by changing one number and rerunning.

And one about significant figures. Several sourced values look more exact than they are, because they are round numbers in pounds and feet converted to metric: 4,893 N is 1,100 lbf, 64,054 N is 14,400 lbf, 1,627 N m is 1,200 ft lbf, 474.5 N m is 350 ft lbf, and 0.031 m/s is 0.1 ft/s. They are good to two or three figures, not four or five.

**Weight and mass.** In orbit nothing has weight, so nothing presses on anything by itself; every contact force comes from a motor or from motion. Mass is unchanged and matters as much as ever: it sets how much a push changes an object's motion. The vehicle (7,000 kg) is about five times the arm's moving mass (about 1,400 kg, a guess), so the same push moves the arm far more than the vehicle.

## Definitions

A capture has three stages, and "captured" means all three plus latched:

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Engaged | The pin was inside the snare when it finished closing |
| Seated | Pull-in finished with the fixture plate against the rim, on axis and at rest |
| Retained | The latched joint survives 1,627 N m of bending for 5 s with at most 3° of tilt. The load is borrowed from the Shuttle end effector |

What is measured is where *this controller with this mechanism* reaches, snares, pulls in, and holds. That is not the mechanism's geometric tolerance, and it is not what a flight procedure would allow.

## Outside the scope

- **Station safety.** The station is scenery. Nothing collides with it, so "out of reach" is a statement about the controller and the arm's workspace, not a safety judgement.
- **Flight validation.** There is no comparison with telemetry or hardware tests.
- **Cable mechanics.** The snare is three rigid bars, not cables.
- **Fixture cams.** Not modelled. The end effector's four latches are represented only by their total clamping force.
- **Arm flexibility.** The arm is rigid.
- **Orbital mechanics.** Gravity is zero throughout. This is a short-timescale approximation; see the research notes.
- **A human operator.** The real capture is flown by an astronaut. The model's controller is automatic.

## The experiments

Run on 2026-10-11 and 12, and trimmed afterwards to the conditions that carry the answer. A is *how to tell* whether a result is physics. B against C is *what the result is sensitive to*.

**Set-up.** Every condition changes one thing and runs the same 240 attempts: a drift sweep at 4, 8, 12, and 16 cm/s, 30 scenarios each, two seeds, tumble up to 2 deg/s. Each is compared with the baseline attempt by attempt. The baseline captures 110 of 240.

**Experiment A: artefact checks.** Every attempt in every batch is flagged if a step goes unstable, a contact force on the vehicle passes 150 kN, the vehicle leaves a contact faster than 1.5 times its fastest speed before, two shapes overlap by more than 1 cm, or a sliding joint goes 5 mm past its stop. Tested on the old rim, where 50 attempts are known artefacts: all 50 flagged.

**Experiment B: the same object, represented differently.**

| What changes | Settings | Outcomes changed, of 240 |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing meaningful (start nudged 0.001 mm) | | 3: the noise floor |
| The rim's collision shape | box tops | 79 |
| | 16 or 64 spheres, against 32 | 2, 0 |
| The cavity wall's collision shape | 8 or 32 boxes, against 16 | 5, 7 |
| Time step | 0.5 ms, against 2 ms | 3 |
| Contact stiffness | twice as stiff | 7 |

**Experiment C: physical numbers that are not known exactly.**

| Number | In the model | Range tried | Captured at each end | Outcomes changed, of 240 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joint rate cap | 0.03 rad/s | 0.015 to 0.06 | 21, 199 | 180 |
| Friction where the fixture seats on the rim | 0.3 | 0.1 to 0.8 | 111, 14 | 96 |
| Observation delay | 0 | 0.5, 1.0 s | 101, 55 | 68 |
| Joint back-drive friction | 200 N m | 100 to 400 | 111, 101 | 17 |
| Friction where the snare holds the pin | 0.3 | 0.1 to 0.8 | 111, 108 | 8 |
| Snare drive force (control: expected to do nothing) | 1,500 N | 750 to 3,000 | 108, 110 | 2 |

**Taken out after running once.** These made no difference worth keeping a condition for, and are recorded here so the result is not lost: pull-in force from 3,560 to 6,100 N (1 outcome changed), vehicle mass from 5,000 to 8,050 kg (8), a 1 ms time step (2), and friction changed everywhere at once (the same as changing it at the rim).

**The answer, for this model.** Once the checks pass, a sound change of representation moves the result about as much as noise (at most 7 of 240), and the physical unknowns move it up to 180. One unsound representation, the box-top rim, moved it 79, and nothing but the checks showed that it was unsound.

## Decisions

The ones that shape the project. Superseded and minor decisions are in the git history.

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| Use existing simulators, not a from-scratch engine | More to learn from the tools the field uses |
| Reconstruct a real operation | Real numbers to check against, and real consequences to reason about |
| MuJoCo, on a MacBook, CPU only | No NVIDIA GPU; the task is rigid bodies, which MuJoCo handles well |
| Detailed 3D models are appearance only; collisions use simple shapes | Fast and predictable contact, and the simulation runs without the downloaded files |
| Hollow end effector built from a ring of 16 boxes | MuJoCo collides convex shapes, so a cavity cannot be one shape |
| Snare modelled as three rigid bars | Catches and centres the pin without simulating cables. Its limits are now known: see Risks |
| Flat-backed knob on the pin, not a ball | The ball wedged the bars apart and slipped out |
| Fixture plate seats on a ring of 32 spheres | The box tops gave it redundant contacts that broke the solver |
| Arm tracks until the snare has closed, then goes limp for pull-in | Stopping at the trigger let a receding pin escape; the real arm is limp while the end effector pulls itself straight |
| The controller sees the pin through a sensor model, smoothed by an estimator | To ask how capture degrades with stale or noisy observations |
| Six outcome labels, and "captured" requires seated, latched, and retained | A single success flag hides why attempts fail |
| Scenarios are plain data generated from a seed; experiments change one thing on the same scenarios | Reproducible, and attempts can be compared in pairs |
| Every research claim is labelled Read, Reported, Assumed, or Disputed | A summary of a source is not the source |
| One general question: is a contact-rich result physics or artefact, and what is it sensitive to? | The capture solves no problem for the space industry; the answer should carry to physical AI and physics simulation |
| Every attempt is checked for signs it is not physics | Outcome labels alone hid a contact failure for four milestones |
| I build, with Claude Code explaining and reviewing | The point is to understand the system well enough to explain it |

## Assumptions with no source

- How the two boom halves mate at the elbow (which side boom B sits on).
- Arm link masses and inertias, totalling about 1,400 kg of moving mass.
- Joint servo gains, damping, and reflected inertia. No torque limits. A 0.03 rad/s (1.7°/s) joint rate cap.
- Vehicle mass split between modules. The grapple pin's position is where the model shows a small fixture, which is probably the grapple fixture.
- Grapple pin: 0.3 m long, 2 cm radius, with a flat-backed knob. Fixture plate: 0.25 m radius. With the cavity's 0.2 m inner radius these let the plate's edge reach the rim at 5 to 14° of tilt. Canadarm2's end effector was tested at 15°.
- Rim: 32 spheres of 2 cm radius on a 0.22 m circle. A contact surface chosen for the solver's sake, not a model of the real rim.
- Snare: three rigid bars, 6 mm radius, drive force limit 1,500 N. Under a straight pull of about 8,000 N the bars are forced apart and the pin escapes; real cables would not open that way. The bars' open stop and the coupling that makes the three move together are soft constraints: in lost attempts the bars were pushed up to 4 cm past the stop and up to 7.7 cm apart from each other. The pilots showed the drive's force limit does not set this, so what does is an open question about the model.
- Hover distance: the end effector starts 2 m from the pin.
- Which point the published 10 m is measured from; here it is the module surface under the arm's base.
- Sensor update rate and noise levels. Velocities reach the controller delayed but without added error.
- Tumble rates in free drift; batches use up to 0.5°/s by default.
- Controller gains, 3 cm/s insertion speed, alignment tolerances, estimator gain of 0.2, abort threshold.
- Station model scale of 2.35 and the arm's mounting position on it.

## Risks

- **Results describe the substitute, not the hardware.** Rigid bars for cables, a force for the latches, a plate on spheres for the cams. The rigid bars are known to wedge the pin's knob with up to 90 kN at low friction.
- **The controller is part of the result.** The largest sensitivity found, the joint rate cap, is a property of it.
- **The arm's motors have no torque limit.** When the end effector runs into the hull, they push with up to 200,000 N m. There is no "collided" outcome yet.
- **Tilt is removed by a lever with unsourced dimensions.** Plate radius, rim radius, and pin length set the tilt at which the plate meets the rim, and the result depends on friction there.
- **The original contact failure has a likely cause, not a proved one.** Other contacts between cylinders and overlapping boxes remain, the knob inside the cavity among them.
- **Zero gravity is a timescale assumption.** Reasonable for a one-minute attempt; not shown to be negligible against centimetre tolerances.
