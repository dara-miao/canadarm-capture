## The question

When a space station arm reaches out to grab a free-floating cargo vehicle, how much error can it tolerate before the capture fails, and which physical parameters decide that?

## Why this project

Free-flyer capture is a real operation: Canadarm2 grapples vehicles such as Cygnus while they drift with thrusters off. Nothing holds the vehicle still, so a bad contact pushes it away. That makes the outcome depend on contact geometry, friction, compliance, and actuator behaviour, which are the same parameters that decide contact-rich manipulation on Earth.

The project is simulation only. It is a reasoned study of a real operation, not a validated replica.

## Decisions

| Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-09 | Use existing simulators, not a from-scratch engine | More to learn from the tools the field uses, and a stronger result for the same effort |
| 2026-10-10 | Reconstruct a real operation, not a generic pick-and-place | A generic arm demo does not stand out |
| 2026-10-10 | Everything runs on a MacBook (CPU only) | No NVIDIA GPU available; rules out Isaac Sim and the Omniverse runtime libraries |
| 2026-10-10 | MuJoCo first, Newton as a second engine later | The task is rigid-body only; MuJoCo is mature on macOS and easy to debug |
| 2026-10-10 | Build the hollow end effector from a ring of 16 boxes | MuJoCo collides convex shapes, so a cavity cannot be one mesh |
| 2026-10-10 | Model the snare as three rigid bars forming a shrinking triangle | Real snares are cables; rigid bars reproduce the centring and catching action without cable simulation |
| 2026-10-10 | Open snare is the triangle inscribed in the cavity (inradius 0.1 m) | Matches the published lateral capture tolerance of ±0.1 m for this end-effector design |
| 2026-10-10 | Flat-backed knob on the pin tip, not a ball | A ball wedged the bars apart under load and the pin slipped out |
| 2026-10-10 | Stiff, high-force snare drive | The real drive is geared and is not pushed back open by load on the pin |
| 2026-10-10 | Resolved-rate control through the damped Jacobian pseudo-inverse | Standard for redundant arms; needs no trajectory planner for a short approach |
| 2026-10-10 | Leave roll about the pin axis uncontrolled | The modelled pin is symmetric; the real fixture has alignment cams that this model omits |
| 2026-10-10 | Arm freezes when the snare fires | Simple and close to stopping the arm at capture; means any remaining drift is absorbed by the snare and the joint servos |
| 2026-10-10 | Controller reads the true pin pose and velocity | Sensing error and delay become explicit parameters in experiment B instead of being hidden in a perception model |
| 2026-10-10 | Insert a realism pass before the trial harness | The experiments are only as credible as the model they run on |
| 2026-10-10 | Fetch the 3D models with a script; do not store them in the repository | The Canadian Space Agency permits non-commercial reproduction with credit; fetching from the source avoids redistributing the files |
| 2026-10-10 | Rebuild the arm's joints from the CSA model's separate parts | The model shows Canadarm2's offset joints, which the first straight-line arm did not have |
| 2026-10-10 | Meshes are appearance only; collisions keep simple shapes | Keeps contact fast and predictable, and the simulation runs without the downloaded files |
| 2026-10-10 | Mount the arm on the Earth-facing side of the forward node (Harmony) | Cygnus is captured about 10 m below the station; the exact fixture used is not sourced, so this is an approximation |
| 2026-10-10 | Move the grapple pin to the service module | The Cygnus model has a small fixture there |
| 2026-10-10 | Use the Shuttle arm's end-effector paper as the main parameter source | It is the only primary source found with numbers; Canadarm2's end effector is a larger descendant of the same design, so the values are indicative, not exact |
| 2026-10-10 | Pull-in force limit 3,560 N | The paper's rigidize load in the grapple shaft |
| 2026-10-10 | Arm goes limp during pull-in, except wrist roll | The paper says angular misalignment is removed against a limped arm |
| 2026-10-10 | Arm keeps tracking until the snare has closed | Stopping at the trigger failed at the paper's 0.031 m/s relative velocity |
| 2026-10-10 | Cap tip speed at 0.37 m/s | Canadarm2's published unloaded speed |
| 2026-10-10 | Every attempt starts with the pin at the 10 m capture point and the arm hovering 2 m short | Matches the published capture distance; the hover distance is an assumption |
| 2026-10-10 | Five outcome labels: captured, caught but not rigid, missed, out of reach, timed out | A single success flag hides why attempts fail |
| 2026-10-10 | Controller aborts when the arm's Jacobian is nearly singular | Without it the arm thrashed at the edge of its workspace |
| 2026-10-10 | Controller sees the pin only through a sensor model with delay, update rate, and noise | With perfect knowledge every attempt succeeds and the experiments show nothing |
| 2026-10-10 | Controller smooths measurements with a fixed-gain estimator | Checking alignment against raw noisy readings meant it never committed to inserting |
| 2026-10-10 | Scenarios are plain data generated from a seed | Batches are reproducible and can be spread across processes |
| 2026-10-10 | Pause before the experiments to build up the research notes | Results are only worth reporting if the model's inputs and limits are understood first |
| 2026-10-10 | Label every research claim Read, Reported, Assumed, or Disputed | Secondary summaries are useful for finding sources but are not sources |
| 2026-10-10 | Treat the station as fixed and the arm as rigid | The station is about 60 times heavier than the vehicle; arm flexibility is left out to keep the first version simple |

Daniell and Sachdev (1982) describes the Shuttle arm's end effector: 0.34 m in diameter, 0.46 m long, 29.5 kg. Canadarm2's is a larger descendant with added latches, so these values are indicative for it, not exact.

## Assumptions still unsourced

- Arm joint positions: measured from the bounding boxes of the parts in the CSA 1:100 model, good to a few centimetres at best. Each boom is 7.04 m between pitch axes.
- How the two boom halves mate at the elbow (which side boom B sits on) is a guess.
- Arm link masses and inertias: invented, totalling about 1,400 kg of moving mass.
- Joint servo gains, damping, and reflected inertia: invented for stable tracking. No torque limits. A 0.03 rad/s joint rate cap is invented.
- Arm is rigid; the real one flexes.
- Vehicle: collision shape is two cylinders fitted to the CSA Cygnus model; mass split by guess. The grapple pin sits where the model shows a small fixture on the service module, which is probably the grapple fixture but is not confirmed.
- Grapple pin: 0.3 m long, 2 cm radius, with a flat-backed knob. The real fixture has a shaft with cam arms, which are not modelled.
- Snare: three rigid bars, 6 mm radius, drive force limit 1,500 N, friction 0.6. Cable tension and friction are not sourced.
- Hover distance: the end effector starts 2 m from the pin. No source found for the real pre-capture distance.
- Which point the published 10 m is measured from is not stated; here it is the module surface under the arm's base.
- Sensing: delay, update rate, and noise levels are free parameters. Only the 0.3 to 0.5 s ground-control latency is sourced. Velocities reach the controller delayed but without added error.
- Tumble rates for a vehicle in free drift are not sourced; batches use up to 0.5°/s by default.
- Controller: gains, 3 cm/s insertion speed, alignment tolerances, estimator gain of 0.2, abort threshold. Real captures are flown by an operator; the controller here has perfect knowledge of the pin.
- Station: NASA's model (B), scaled by 2.35. Low-detail, untextured, US modules about 15% narrow. The arm's mounting position on the forward node is approximate.

## Risks

- **Snare modelling.** Resolved for now with rigid bars (milestone 2). The bars cannot stretch or wrap like cables, which may matter for angular misalignment.
- **The envelope depends on invented limits.** With sensing modelled, what bounds success is the arm's joint rate cap, the alignment tolerances, and the estimator gain, all of which are invented. Experiment results must be reported as conditional on them.
- **Off-axis loads.** With the pin 2.5 m from the vehicle's centre of mass, a force through the centre of mass levers the fixture sideways. The seated capture resists about 850 N m of bending, which meets the 474.5 N m no-separation figure but not the 1,627 N m case; the real fixture's cam arms and the end effector's latches are missing.
- **Contact tuning.** Stiff contact in zero gravity may need a smaller timestep or solver tuning.
- **Newton on macOS.** Supported in CPU-only mode but untested here.
